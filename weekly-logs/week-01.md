## One concept I can now explain
**Compensating controls:** When the ideal security control 
isn't possible due to a constraint, you implement an 
alternative that covers the gap. A hospital can't patch 
legacy medical equipment without breaking it, so they 
isolate it on a separate VLAN with strict firewall rules 
instead. The key pattern: "cannot do X, so instead they 
do Y" — that's always compensating.

## What confused me and how I resolved it
Almost misidentified a compensating control as preventive 
because the VLAN firewall does have a preventive effect. 
Resolved it by understanding that control type depends on 
*context and intent*, not just what the control does in 
isolation. When a scenario describes a constraint first, 
compensating is almost always the answer.
