## Most significant concept I learned
Shadow IT are systems, tools or services used without the approval of an organization. Not always for malicious gain - an employee might use Dropbox to share files to teams because the approval process is slow. The risk is it’s outside the organization’s security control.


## Most useful confusion I resolved
Defined ethical as attacking with permission to identify possible vulnerabilities and patching them before real attackers do. That's what an ethical hacker does (a job description) not ethical as the motivation. Ethical as a motivation is attacking and morally justifying the attack - a whistleblower leaking classified documents because they believe the public deserves to know, or someone attacking a company they believe is causing harm.

Corrections from re-audit:
"Ethical" as a motivation in the exam context doesn't mean ethical hacking or penetration testing with permission. It means an attacker who genuinely believes their attack is morally justified. It's motivation, not a job description. Ethical hacking is a profession. "Ethical" as a motivation is about moral justification for an otherwise unauthorized act.

While hacktivists are the most common actors with the "philosophical/political beliefs" motivation, they're not the only ones - nation-states also act on political beliefs, insiders sometimes act on ideological grounds. The motivation category cuts across multiple actor types.
